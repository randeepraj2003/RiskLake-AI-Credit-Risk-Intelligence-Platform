SECTION 1: PROBABILITY OF DEFAULT (PD) INTERPRETATION

1.1 Risk Grade Definitions
RiskLake uses a five-grade PD model aligned with Basel II IRB (Internal Ratings-Based) approach:

Grade A — PD < 5%
  Interpretation: Very low default risk. Borrower demonstrates strong debt serviceability.
  Policy action: Approve at standard rate. No additional conditions required.

Grade B — PD 5% to 14.99%
  Interpretation: Low default risk. Borrower is creditworthy with minor risk signals.
  Policy action: Approve at standard or slightly elevated rate. Quarterly monitoring.

Grade C — PD 15% to 29.99%
  Interpretation: Moderate default risk. One or more risk signals present.
  Policy action: Approve with conditions — collateral verification, income proof, semi-annual review.
  Typical triggers: Elevated DTI, moderate credit utilisation, borderline credit score.

Grade D — PD 30% to 49.99%
  Interpretation: High default risk. Multiple risk signals present.
  Policy action: Senior credit officer review required. Reduced loan amount or enhanced collateral.
  Typical triggers: DTI above 43%, high credit utilisation, delinquency history, irregular EMI payments.

Grade E — PD >= 50%
  Interpretation: Very high default risk. Borrower unlikely to service debt.
  Policy action: Decline or refer to secured loan product with 200% collateral coverage.

1.2 Model Confidence and SHAP Interpretation
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values indicate the contribution of each feature to the model's PD prediction relative to the average prediction (base rate).

- Positive SHAP value: Feature pushes PD probability ABOVE the population average (increases default risk).
- Negative SHAP value: Feature pulls PD probability BELOW the population average (decreases default risk).
- SHAP magnitude: Larger absolute values indicate stronger feature influence.

Key features and their typical SHAP interpretation:
- dti_ratio (positive SHAP): High debt burden relative to income. Primary driver of default in retail credit.
- credit_stress_score (positive SHAP): Composite of inquiry velocity, balance trend, EMI irregularity. Strong behavioural signal.
- emi_regularity_score (negative SHAP): Consistent EMI payment history is the strongest protective factor.
- bureau_utilisation_pct (positive SHAP): High credit card utilisation indicates financial stress.
- collateral_coverage_ratio (negative SHAP): Strong collateral reduces expected loss even if default occurs.
- delinquency_rate (positive SHAP): History of missed payments is a significant predictor of future default.

SECTION 2: CREDIT UTILISATION RISK SIGNALS

2.1 Utilisation Thresholds
- Utilisation < 30%: Healthy credit management. No risk adjustment.
- Utilisation 30-60%: Moderate utilisation. Monitor for upward trend.
- Utilisation 60-90%: High utilisation. Indicative of credit dependency. Apply risk premium.
- Utilisation > 90% (maxed_out): Severe financial stress signal. Requires explanation and decline consideration.

2.2 Utilisation Trend vs Bureau Snapshot
If the transaction-derived utilisation estimate exceeds the bureau snapshot by more than 15 percentage points (utilisation_change_flag = 1), this may indicate:
  a) The bureau data is stale and actual utilisation has increased since last pull.
  b) The customer has recently taken on new credit not yet reflected in the bureau.
  c) A seasonal spend spike unrelated to chronic financial stress.
Action: Manual review recommended. Request updated bureau pull before final decision.

2.3 EMI Regularity Score
- Score >= 0.9: Excellent repayment discipline. Strongest protective SHAP signal.
- Score 0.7-0.89: Good repayment. Minor gaps acceptable with documentation.
- Score 0.5-0.69: Moderate irregularity. Investigate cause before approval.
- Score < 0.5: Poor repayment discipline. High correlation with future default. Decline or secure.

SECTION 3: BEHAVIOURAL RISK SIGNALS FROM TRANSACTIONS

3.1 Credit Stress Score Interpretation
The credit_stress_score (0-4) is an additive composite of four binary flags:
  +1 if high_inquiry_flag = 1     (3+ hard inquiries in 6 months)
  +1 if balance_trend_flag = 1    (recent 3-month balance > earlier 9-month balance)
  +1 if emi_regularity_score < 0.5 (poor EMI payment discipline)
  +1 if flagged_txn_rate > 5%     (>5% of transactions flagged as suspicious)

Score interpretation:
  0: No stress signals. Proceed normally.
  1: Minor stress. Note in file. Standard approval process.
  2: Moderate stress. Require documentation of each flag. Semi-annual review.
  3: High stress. Senior credit officer approval. Reduced loan amount.
  4: Critical stress. Decline unless exceptional compensating factors.

3.2 Flagged Transaction Rate
- Rate 0-2%: Normal. No action.
- Rate 2-5%: Monitor. Request statement explanation if rate is recent.
- Rate > 5%: Flag for compliance review. Potential AML concern. Do not approve until cleared.

SECTION 4: COMPENSATING FACTORS

When a borrower presents borderline risk signals, the following compensating factors may allow approval:

4.1 Strong Compensating Factors (can offset one full risk tier)
- Collateral coverage ratio >= 2.0
- EMI regularity score >= 0.95 over 24+ months
- Credit score >= 800 with credit history > 10 years
- Loan-to-income ratio <= 1.0

4.2 Moderate Compensating Factors (can offset within a tier)
- Long-standing customer relationship (> 5 years)
- Salary credited directly to bank account for > 2 years
- Co-applicant with clean credit history and independent income

4.3 Factors That Cannot Be Offset
- Active default in the last 12 months
- Ongoing legal proceedings related to debt
- Flagged transaction rate > 10% without AML clearance
